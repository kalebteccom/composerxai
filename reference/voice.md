# Voice

Loaded by every register except [reference](../skills/composing/registers/reference.md), where
sounding mechanical is correct.

## The seven grammatical rules

These catch what a word list never will, because they name the mechanic instead of the symptom.

**1. No maxims.** Say what happened to *this* thing. Never close on a general truth about the
category. "Rules without teeth do not happen" is a maxim. Cut it.

**2. No see-saw closers.** `X rather than Y`, `X, not Y`, `not just X but Y`. Two clauses of similar
length where the second restates the first from the negative side. It has the shape of a conclusion,
which is why it reads as wise while carrying nothing. State X. Stop.

**3. No definite article on a noun not yet introduced.** "The scope shift" when no scope shift was
mentioned presupposes shared knowledge, so the reader accepts a framing before being given it. New
things take `a`, or get named.

**4. Simple past for past events.** `marked`, not `was marking`. The progressive does no aspectual
work here; it spreads the claim out so it sounds considered.

**5. Name the referent.** No `that job`, `this pattern`, `that asymmetry`, `the thing to defend`.

**6. Objects have no intentions or strength.** A comma does not do a job. Columns do not carry nuance.

**7. One idea per sentence.** If it balances on `, and`, split it and cut the decorative half.

**The engine behind most of it is a tense shift**: reporting a specific past event, then sliding
mid-sentence into present-simple pronouncement about the general case. That single move does more to
make prose sound machine-written than any word choice.

## Structure

- **No rule of three.** Three items because three feels complete. Use the number there actually are.
- **No dramatic one-line paragraph.** Vary paragraph length because the content varies, not for effect.
- **No closing summary** restating what was just said. Stop at the last real point.
- **No signposting**: `it's worth noting`, `importantly`, `that said`, `here's the thing`. If it is
  worth noting, note it.
- **Do not hedge both sides.** "While X is true, Y is also worth considering." Pick one, say why the
  other loses.
- **Vary paragraph length genuinely.** Uniform blocks are a tell.

## Words

Cut: `delve, leverage, utilize, foster, empower, elevate, unlock, unleash, showcase, streamline,
facilitate, underscore, embark, spearhead, harness, robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge,
transformative, pivotal, crucial, invaluable, meticulous, holistic, impactful, unparalleled, myriad,
landscape, realm, journey, tapestry, testament, plethora, synergy, cornerstone, deep dive`.

Openers that mark a letter as generated: `I am writing to`, `I hope this email finds you well`,
`As a seasoned`, `With over X years of experience`.

⚠️ **Two rules deliberately absent, both because the evidence says they are wrong.**

**No passive-voice check.** Measured, GPT-4o uses *less* agentless passive than humans, roughly half.
Flagging it makes the tool confidently wrong.

**`delve` is opt-in, not default.** The corpus evidence for it is solid, but flagging it carries a
measured bias against people who did not learn English first.

## Numbers over adjectives

`4,320 users`, not `a significant number of users`. If the number is unknown, say it is unknown.

## Calibrated confidence

Do not hedge into mush. Say what is known, how it is known, and what would change the answer.
`Measured` and `assumed` are different words and the difference matters more than any softener.
